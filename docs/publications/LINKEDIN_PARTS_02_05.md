## Part 2 — Different roles need different evidence

**Attachment:** `assets/publication/linkedin-02-permitted-views.png`

**Alt text:** Trace, a blue geometric firefly, passes through a permission gate. One connected account map becomes two permitted evidence paths while protected records remain behind the gate.

```text
One customer account can support two very different decisions.

Before a customer call, a salesperson needs what was agreed and the next step.

Marketing needs an approved customer signal it can use in a campaign.

Both people need the same account map. Neither needs every private note.

That is why I do not think personalization starts with a longer summary. It starts with the decision a person needs to make today.

The goal is one shared source of context, with each person seeing permitted evidence, its source, how current it is, and what is still missing. The system can point to a next step. The person remains responsible for the decision.

In Part 2 of SalesWiki, I explain the practical rule behind this: shared context should not mean unrestricted context.
```

## Part 3 — Start with one decision

**Attachment:** `assets/publication/linkedin-03-decision-card.png`

**Alt text:** Trace stands by one translucent decision card. A clear cyan path leads through evidence checks to an action, while an amber branch ends at a question mark for missing or stale context.

```text
Imagine two customer accounts on your list.

One has a confirmed next step due today. The other is marked high priority, but its last call note is old.

Which one would you follow up with first?

A summary of both accounts may be accurate and still leave that question unanswered.

For Part 3 of my SalesWiki series, I narrowed the experiment to one repeated decision: which account needs attention today, and why? A useful answer should show the dated facts behind a suggestion and say what is missing. The person still decides what to do.

I want to compare that short answer with sources against the usual account notes. If it saves time but leads to a worse choice, it fails the test. The public demo uses invented accounts; whether this helps a real team is still an open question.
```

## Part 4 — A demo is not a pilot

**Attachment:** `assets/publication/linkedin-04-private-pilot.png`

**Alt text:** Trace follows a reviewed path from a synthetic demo card to a separate small private vault. The two data spaces are visibly distinct.

```text
The demo shows an invented customer account. What would have to change before I could test the same workflow with a real one?

First, real records would need a separate private space and clear rules about who can see them. Then I would choose one repeated decision to test, such as which account needs attention today.

I would compare a short answer with visible sources against the team's usual notes and customer records. Did it help someone choose a next step with less searching? Could they explain the choice? What happened when a fact was missing or wrong?

The public SalesWiki demo cannot answer those questions for a real team. That is why Part 4 is about the boundary between a useful demo and a small, careful pilot.
```

## Part 5 — An integration needs a job

**Attachment:** `assets/publication/linkedin-05-evidence-paths.png`

**Alt text:** Trace stands at a central evidence junction. Abstract CRM, document, and chat sources arrive on separate paths; one cited path continues toward a next-action marker.

```text
Imagine a salesperson deciding which customer account to contact today. The next step is in the customer system, a useful detail is in a document, and a chat thread contains an unconfirmed claim.

Should we connect all three to SalesWiki?

I would start with a narrower question: which missing fact could change the decision? A connection earns its place when it supplies that fact with a source, or puts the resulting answer where the person already works.

The original customer record can stay in the customer system. The document can stay in the document store. SalesWiki can link the permitted, dated evidence without making another copy of everything.

Part 5 explains this rule and where I would use a vendor's integration instead of building one more adapter myself.

[DEV article URL after publication]

Which integration in your stack has a clear decision job, and which one mainly creates another copy of the same context?
```
